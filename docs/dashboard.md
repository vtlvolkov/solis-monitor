# Dashboard guide

What each page shows, how the numbers are calculated and which settings in `.env` affect them. For a short tour with pictures see the [README](../README.md#a-tour).

- **Overview**: live power-flow diagram (PV, Grid, Grid Load, Battery, Backup Load), battery SOC bar, today's kWh, a backup-runtime estimate, and a red banner during a grid outage. Its cards can be moved, removed and added: see [Customizing the Overview](#customizing-the-overview).
- **Devices**: inverter, battery, logger and meter cards with status, like the Solis app. Tap a card for details:
  - inverter: serial number, firmware, state, fault codes, clock drift, AC/PV/grid values
  - battery: SOC/SOH, voltage, current, today's kWh, plus BMS voltage/current, battery temperature, BMS charge and discharge limits, BMS failure codes, and for Dyness batteries an estimate of the batteries in parallel (the BMS discharge limit ÷ 100 A; the inverter has no register for the count, so the estimate can read low or disappear while the BMS lowers its limit, and it never changes the saved capacity)
  - meter: voltage, current, active/reactive/apparent power, power factor, frequency, lifetime import/export
  - logger: address, link status, last data

  Device details are read from the inverter by the poller on request (cached 30 s). Their register meanings were checked against a live S6-EH1P.
- **Events**: grid connected or out (with time left on battery), active alarms, outage history (start, end, duration, battery SOC before → after, totals) and alarm history with decoded fault names. The same day picker as on Charts lists one exact day's outages and alarms, with a 24-hour timeline; an event that crosses midnight appears on both days, and the battery energy is an estimate from the SOC change.
- **Settings**: see [Changing inverter settings](settings.md). **General → Inverter connection** holds the logger's address (it can find it on your network; an address saved there wins over `INVERTER_HOST` in `.env`, which is only the first one, and **Use … again** goes back to it), the inverter clock, electricity prices and the storage mode.
- **Charts**: stats, power flows, SOC, battery voltage and current, grid and backup-output voltage, frequency, energy today, an energy summary (today / yesterday / month / year / lifetime), and daily kWh for the last 30 days. The time range is kept in the URL (`?view=charts&range=24h`). Next to the ranges, a day picker opens one exact day (`?view=charts&day=2026-10-24`), from the ‹ › arrows, a calendar that marks the days with outages and alarms, or a click on a bar of the daily chart. The tiles then show that day's energy, peaks, SOC range and outages, and the charts run from midnight to midnight with outages shaded. Day boundaries follow `TZ`, and a day before the first stored reading cannot be picked.
- **Debug** (Settings → Debug): reads registers by hand and shows the request and the inverter's answer, to find out what a register holds. Pick input (function 04) or holding (function 03) registers, an address (decimal or `0x…`) and a count of 1–50. It shows both frames as hex, the round trip time, and a table with each register as hex, unsigned, signed, ASCII and 32-bit values (a register together with the next one, high word first); *Copy as text* puts it on the clipboard for a bug report or a message. A Modbus exception from the inverter (such as "Illegal data address") is shown as the answer it is. It can only read, never write, and needs `WEB_BASIC_AUTH` like the changes do, but not `CONTROL_ENABLED`. The read goes through the poller's single connection between the regular readings.

**Full in** (next to "Charging" on the Battery card) = capacity × (Max charge SOC − SOC) ÷ the average charging power over the last 15 minutes, leaving out moments it was not charging. It moves with clouds and can read short near full, when charging slows. Hidden below 50 W.

**Backup runtime** = usable energy ÷ load, where usable energy is capacity × (SOC − the discharge floor on the backup output, 33298). The load is the average of the last 15 minutes, refreshed every minute. Capacity comes from the *Battery capacity* card on the Settings page (Battery tab), else `BATTERY_CAPACITY_KWH`, else the inverter's rated-Ah setting (43019) × 51.2 V. Set it if you know your battery's usable kWh, and change it on the card when you move a battery to another inverter: it needs `WEB_BASIC_AUTH` like the other changes but not `CONTROL_ENABLED`, since nothing is written to the inverter. The saved value lives in `/data/battery-capacity.json` in the poller's `poller-data` volume; *Use default* removes it and goes back to `.env` or the inverter.

**Outages and alarms** are recorded by the poller into measurement `solis_event`, when the "No grid" fault bit is set, the status is 0x1015, or grid voltage drops below 100 V. A change must be seen in 2 consecutive polls. History starts when you install this version.

The Overview and the TV page show four power gauges as horizontal bars: **Grid** (the size of the grid power in either direction, with an Importing/Exporting note; scale 0–6 kW), **Backup load** (0–6 kW, the inverter's maximum), and **Battery charge** and **Battery discharge** (the battery's power in each direction, 0 while it goes the other way, with a note). The battery bars' maximum follows the **Max charge current** / **Max discharge current** setting times a voltage: the inverter's equalize voltage for charging (40 A × 55 V = 2.2 kW) and its float voltage for discharging (60 A × 53.5 V = 3.21 kW), or the nominal 51.2 V if they can't be read. It changes when you change those settings, and charging at the current limit fills the bar. Each bar turns amber from 60% and red from 85% of its maximum, with a short status under it. On the Overview the bars sit under the power diagram (at the bottom of the page on a phone); on the TV page they are a 2×2 block at the top right. The limits are `WARN_AT`, `DANGER_AT` and the grid bar's `GAUGE_GRID_MAX_W` in `web/frontend/src/lib/gauge.ts`.

Live values arrive over Server-Sent Events every ~10 s. If SSE fails, the page falls back to polling (`?transport=poll` forces polling). The status pill turns red when the newest point is older than 45 s.

| Env var | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `WEB_PORT` | `8088` | Host port |
| `WEB_BASIC_AUTH` | empty | `user:pass` turns on HTTP Basic auth (`/healthz` stays open for the Docker healthcheck). The password can later be changed on Settings → System → Dashboard login; the new one is kept as a hash in `/data/auth.json` and wins until you go back to the original (or run `solis-monitor reset-login`) |
| `SITE_NAME` | empty | Name of this installation: dashboard title, browser tab and a prefix on Telegram alerts |
| `WEB_AUTH_SETTINGS_ONLY` | empty | `true` = only `/api/settings*` needs the password, so the dashboard is open and the browser asks for the username and password when you open the Settings tab. Needs `WEB_BASIC_AUTH`. |
| `TARIFF_*` | empty | Starting electricity prices; change them later on the Settings page (see "Electricity cost" below) |
| `INFLUX_*`, `TZ` | shared | Same values as the poller. `TZ` sets the day boundaries for daily kWh |

**Electricity cost.** Set `TARIFF_DAY` (and, for a two-zone meter, `TARIFF_NIGHT`, `TARIFF_NIGHT_START`, `TARIFF_NIGHT_END`, `TARIFF_CURRENCY`) in `.env` and the Overview shows today's and this month's grid cost, the tariff zone that is active now (from the server's clock) and a day-zone / night-zone table with each zone's kWh and cost. The card's **History** link opens a page for any month since the first stored reading (`?view=cost&month=2026-10&day=2026-10-18`): month totals, a stacked bar chart of every day (night zone and day zone, in cost or kWh), a table of all days, and, for a selected day, the day/night split with its cost, plus the house consumption, export, PV and battery energy. Day and night are split from the grid-import counter every 15 minutes, with the zone hours of the tariff in force at that time. The inverter resets its daily counters at midnight by its own clock, which may differ from the server's by seconds or minutes, so each 15 minutes count only what the counter added, and the reset is recognised by the counter falling (versions before 0.10.2 counted the previous day's import again, as night import, whenever a reading just after midnight still held it).

**Changing the prices.** Use the *Electricity tariff* card on the Settings page (it needs `WEB_BASIC_AUTH`, like the other changes). A change starts at the next quarter-hour, or on a date you pick, and only prices consumption from then on: every earlier 15-minute window keeps the price and the night hours it had, so past days and months do not change (the cost page lists the prices that applied in the month). A newer save replaces changes that were scheduled to start later. The currency stays the one in `.env`. The `TARIFF_*` values in `.env` are the prices before your first change; after that the saved list (`/data/tariff.json` in the `web-data` Docker volume) is the source and `.env` is ignored. Each site keeps its own. To start over, delete that file and restart the web service.

MCP (agents): `/mcp`, see [Alerts and AI agents](alerts-and-agents.md). API (GET): `/api/live`, `/api/stream` (SSE), `/api/history?fields=a,b&range=6h&every=auto&agg=mean`, `/api/energy/daily?days=30`, `/api/energy/summary`, `/api/cost/history?month=2026-10`, `/api/events?days=30`, `/api/day/history?date=2026-10-24&fields=a,b`, `/api/day/energy?date=2026-10-24`, `/api/day/events?date=2026-10-24`, `/api/events/calendar?month=2026-10`, `/api/devices`, `/api/settings`, `/api/settings/log`, `/healthz`. `POST /api/settings` changes a setting.
Ranges: `1h 3h 6h 12h 24h 2d 7d 30d`. `every=auto` keeps each series at 500 points or fewer.

**Language:** the dashboard is available in English and Ukrainian. The **EN / УК** button in the page header switches it and remembers the choice in the browser. Without a saved choice it follows the browser or TV language, and `?lang=uk` / `?lang=en` in the URL forces one. Fault and alarm names from the inverter are translated too; a name with no translation is shown in English. Telegram alerts are not translated (yet). To add a string: wrap it in `t('English text')` and add the Ukrainian text to `web/frontend/src/lib/i18n.uk.ts` (a test fails if one is missing).

Code: `web/backend` (FastAPI) and `web/frontend` (Vite + Preact + uPlot). `web/Dockerfile` builds both into one image.

## Customizing the Overview

**Customize** in the Overview's header turns the cards into a layout you can change: drag a card by its **⠿ Move** handle (mouse or finger) within its column or to the other one, remove it with **✕**, and bring cards back or add new ones with **+ Add cards**. With the keyboard, focus the handle and use the arrow keys. **Save** keeps the layout, **Cancel** forgets every change since you pressed Customize, and **Reset to default** brings back the original page. On a phone the two columns stack, left first.

The layout is kept on the server (`/data/overview-layout.json` in the `web-data` volume, `LAYOUT_FILE`), so every screen and browser shows the same page; open screens pick up a change within five minutes. Changing it needs the Settings login (`WEB_BASIC_AUTH`, the browser asks when you press Customize), but not `CONTROL_ENABLED`: nothing goes to the inverter. A layout that matches the default is saved as "default", so cards a later version adds to the default still appear. The TV page keeps its own fixed layout.

Cards you can add, besides the usual ones:

- **Power today**: PV and load from midnight to midnight.
- **Self-sufficiency**: the share of today's energy that came from your PV, PV ÷ (PV + grid import), and the same for this month. Charging the battery from the grid at night counts as grid energy.
- **Recent events**: the last five outages and alarms, with their length.
- **Battery health**: state of health and its change over 30 days, full cycles (lifetime discharge ÷ capacity, an equivalent count), the lifetime round trip (energy out ÷ energy in, the losses of the battery and inverter), the temperature range over 30 days and the usable capacity.
- **Load by hour**: the last 7 days as a heatmap of kWh per hour, and the busiest hour on average.

A card only asks for its data while it is on the page.

## Planned outages (графік відключень)

**Settings → General → Outage schedule** finds your outage group from your address: pick the source (Yasno for Kyiv, or Dnipro DnEM / CEK), start typing the street, pick it, type the house number and pick it; Yasno answers with the group (for example 20.1). For **DTEK Kyiv Oblast, Dnipro and Oblast, and Odesa and Oblast**, pick the group from the list instead: DTEK's regional sites block servers, so there is no address lookup here, but the card links to the site's own address search, which works in your browser. Their schedule comes from [outage-data-ua](https://github.com/Baskerville42/outage-data-ua), a community project on GitHub that copies DTEK's sites every few minutes (downloaded from raw.githubusercontent.com every 15 minutes; it marks half-hour outages, and the card says when the copy is more than three hours old). Only the group is saved, never the address. If you know your group, **I know my group** lets you type it instead. While this is on, the poller asks Yasno (app.yasno.ua, DTEK's own service for those cities) for the group's schedule every 15 minutes; nothing else is sent, and the last good copy is kept, so a Yasno hiccup does not empty the card.

The **Planned outages** card on the Overview (remove or add it with Customize; **Show the Planned outages card on the TV page** on the same Settings card does the same for the TV page, and also decides the Overview until a layout is saved; the schedule and Telegram messages carry on either way) shows the next outage and how long it lasts, whether the battery will carry the house through it, and today and tomorrow as 24-hour bars (red: outage, striped: possible outage, white line: now). The battery verdict starts from the charge expected at the start of the outage (the current charging or discharging carried on until then, up to the maximum charge SOC), takes the energy above the backup floor, and spends it minute by minute at your usual load for those hours (the average of the last two weeks), so a heavy evening counts.

In Telegram (each can be turned off on the same card): 30 minutes before a planned outage, with the battery level and the runtime; once, up to three hours ahead, when the battery will not last through the next outage; and when tomorrow's schedule is published or a schedule changes.

Yasno has no documented API: this uses the JSON its website loads, as the Home Assistant integrations do. If Yasno changes it, the card says it cannot reach the schedule and keeps the last one.

## Grid quality

**Events → Grid quality** judges the supply against the standard (EN 50160 / ДСТУ: 230 V ±10 %, so 207–253 V, and 50 Hz ±0.2 Hz) over the last 24 hours, 7 or 30 days, or one exact day from the day picker (the same one as on Charts and Events; the URL carries `?day=`): the share of time outside the band and the minutes it adds up to, the number of sags (below the band) and surges (above it), the frequency's excursions, and the outages of the period. The voltage and frequency distributions show where the readings sit, the **voltage by hour of day** chart shows when the grid is weak (a Kyiv evening is usually visible), and the day-by-day table has the worst days highlighted. Readings taken during an outage (0 V) are left out; outages are counted from the event log instead. Everything is computed in the database from the 10-second readings, so a month is a few seconds on a Raspberry Pi the first time and cached for five minutes after that.

**Limits** (top right) lets you judge against other numbers than the standard's — for example a 220 V ±10 % band, or the wider 49.5–50.5 Hz. They are kept in your browser and every figure on the page is recomputed with them; **Back to the standard** restores the defaults.

## Export to CSV

**Charts → Export CSV** opens a dialog: the period (the range shown, or a span of days), the step (10 s raw readings up to one day, 1 min up to a week, 15 min, 1 h, or one row per day with the daily kWh totals), which columns (power; battery; grid; energy counters; status and faults) and the format: standard CSV, or the Excel variant with semicolons and a decimal comma that a Ukrainian or European Excel opens directly. Every row starts with the local time (ISO 8601) and the Unix time. The file is streamed from the server, so a month at 15 minutes is as quick as an hour at 10 seconds.

**Events → Export CSV** gives the outages and alarms of the shown period (or the picked day) as rows: kind, name, severity, start, end, whether it is still going on, duration, battery before and after, load at the start. **Cost → Export CSV** gives the shown month, one row per day: consumption, day and night zone, cost, what the battery saved, and the other daily counters.

These downloads are read-only and open like the rest of the data (no Settings login).
